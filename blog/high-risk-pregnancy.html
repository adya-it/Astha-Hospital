Pregnancy

What is a high-risk pregnancy and how is it managed?

By the Astha Hospital team · 2026-11-24

A high-risk pregnancy is one where the mother, the baby or both have a higher chance of complications. Being labelled high risk does not mean something will go wrong. It means the pregnancy needs closer watching and planned care so that problems are caught early.

Common reasons a pregnancy is considered high risk

  • Mother's age below 18 or above 35.
  • High blood pressure, diabetes (including diabetes that starts in pregnancy), thyroid disorders, heart or kidney disease.
  • Twins or more babies.
  • Previous miscarriages, stillbirth, premature birth or caesarean section.
  • Severe anaemia, or low or very high body weight.
  • Pregnancy after fertility treatment.

How care is different

  • More frequent visits so blood pressure, weight and blood sugar can be tracked.
  • Additional blood tests and ultrasound scans to check the baby's growth and well-being.
  • Medicines and diet advice tailored to your condition.
  • A delivery plan made in advance, with the right facilities available, including intensive care for mother and newborn if needed.

What you can do

  • Register your pregnancy early and attend every visit.
  • Take iron, calcium and folic acid exactly as advised.
  • Eat regular, balanced meals and drink enough water.
  • Never stop a long-term medicine without asking your doctor.
  • Know the warning signs and come in early if you notice them.

Delivering at a hospital that has 24x7 emergency care, an ICU and specialist doctors available gives high-risk mothers an important safety margin.
