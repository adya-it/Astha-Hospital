Warning signs in pregnancy: when to call your doctor straight away

By the Astha Hospital team · 2026-11-24

Most pregnancies go smoothly, but some symptoms should never be ignored. Knowing them can protect both mother and baby. If you notice any of the signs below, contact your doctor or come to the hospital without waiting for your next scheduled visit.

Go to the hospital urgently if you have

  • Vaginal bleeding, especially heavy bleeding or bleeding with pain.
  • Severe or persistent headache, blurred vision or flashing lights, particularly in the second half of pregnancy. These can be signs of high blood pressure complications.
  • Sudden swelling of the face, hands or feet, especially together with a headache.
  • Reduced or no movement of the baby once you have started to feel regular movements.
  • Leaking of water from the vagina before labour begins.
  • Severe pain in the abdomen, or regular painful tightening well before your due date.
  • Fever, chills or burning while passing urine.
  • Repeated vomiting so that you cannot keep down food or water.
  • Fits (convulsions) or fainting. Treat this as an emergency.

Do not skip your regular check-ups

Routine antenatal visits are how blood pressure, blood sugar, anaemia and the baby's growth are tracked. Many problems have no obvious symptoms at first, so regular check-ups and scans matter even when you feel well.

Tell your doctor early if

  • You have had a previous miscarriage, premature birth or caesarean section.
  • You have diabetes, thyroid disease, high blood pressure or any long-term illness.
  • You are expecting twins, or you are under 18 or over 35.

These put a pregnancy in the high-risk group, which simply means closer monitoring and extra care. Read more in our article on high-risk pregnancy.
