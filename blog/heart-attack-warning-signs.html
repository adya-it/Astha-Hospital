Heart care

Heart attack warning signs and why the first hour matters

By the Astha Hospital team · 2026-11-24

In a heart attack, a blockage cuts off blood supply to part of the heart muscle. The longer it stays blocked, the more muscle is damaged, so getting to a hospital with round-the-clock emergency and cardiac facilities quickly can save life and heart function.

Warning signs

  • Pressure, tightness, heaviness or squeezing in the centre of the chest, lasting more than a few minutes or coming and going.
  • Pain spreading to the left arm, both arms, jaw, neck, back or upper abdomen.
  • Sweating, breathlessness, nausea or sudden weakness.
  • Women, older people and people with diabetes may have milder or unusual symptoms, such as extreme tiredness, indigestion-like discomfort or breathlessness without obvious chest pain.

What to do

  • Do not wait to see if it passes. Call for emergency help or go to the nearest hospital with emergency and cardiac care immediately.
  • Stop what you are doing and sit down. Avoid driving yourself.
  • Tell the medical team about any heart disease, diabetes or medicines you take.
  • Take aspirin only if your doctor has previously advised it, and you are not allergic.

What are angiography and angioplasty?

Coronary angiography is an imaging test. A thin tube is passed through an artery in the wrist or groin up to the heart, and dye is injected so that blockages show up on X-ray. Angioplasty opens a blocked artery using a tiny balloon, and a small mesh tube called a stent is usually left in place to keep the artery open. These procedures are done in a catheterisation (cath) lab by an interventional cardiologist.

Reduce your risk

  • Control blood pressure, blood sugar and cholesterol, and take regular medicines as prescribed.
  • Do not smoke or chew tobacco.
  • Be active most days and limit salt and fried food.
  • Have regular check-ups if you are over 40 or have a family history of heart disease.
