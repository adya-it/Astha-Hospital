Fertility

Trying for a baby: when should you see a fertility specialist?

By the Astha Hospital team · 2026-11-24

It is normal for a healthy couple to take some months to conceive. But if pregnancy is not happening, the earlier you seek advice, the more options you usually have. Infertility is common, it is a medical condition and it is nothing to be ashamed of.

When to see a specialist

  • Woman under 35: after about 12 months of regular, unprotected intercourse without pregnancy.
  • Woman 35 or older: after about 6 months.
  • Sooner if periods are very irregular or absent, if there is a history of painful periods, endometriosis, pelvic infection, fibroids or previous pelvic surgery, or if the man is known to have a problem.

Both partners should be checked

Causes can lie with the woman, the man, or both, and sometimes no single cause is found. A first visit usually includes a history, an examination, a semen analysis for the man, and for the woman blood tests for hormones, an ultrasound and sometimes a test to check that the tubes are open.

Things that may help while you wait

  • Keep to a healthy weight and eat a balanced diet.
  • Avoid smoking, tobacco and heavy alcohol, for both partners.
  • Take folic acid if you are planning a pregnancy, as advised by your doctor.
  • Have intercourse every two to three days around the middle of the cycle rather than timing it too strictly.
  • Manage stress and get enough sleep.

Treatment

Depending on the cause, treatment can range from lifestyle changes and medicines for ovulation, to keyhole surgery for conditions such as fibroids or endometriosis, to assisted reproduction. A specialist will explain the options that suit your situation.
