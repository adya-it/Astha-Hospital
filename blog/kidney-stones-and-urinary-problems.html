Kidney stones and urinary problems: when to see a urologist

By the Astha Hospital team · 2026-11-24

Urinary problems are common and often treatable, but some need prompt attention. A urologist deals with conditions of the kidneys, bladder and urinary tract in both men and women, and with the male reproductive system.

Kidney stones

Stones form when minerals in urine clump together. Typical symptoms are sudden, severe pain in the side or back that may travel to the lower abdomen or groin, blood in the urine, nausea or vomiting, and burning or frequent urination.

Seek urgent care if you have

  • Severe pain that does not settle with simple pain relief.
  • Pain together with fever or chills, which can mean infection.
  • Inability to pass urine, or repeated vomiting.
  • Blood in the urine.

Other reasons to see a urologist

  • Burning, frequent or urgent urination, or repeated urine infections.
  • Difficulty starting urination, a weak stream or waking many times at night to pass urine.
  • Loss of bladder control.
  • Swelling or pain in the scrotum.

Prevention tips

  • Drink enough water through the day so that your urine stays pale yellow.
  • Limit extra salt, and eat a balanced diet. Ask your doctor about any specific changes for your type of stone.
  • Do not ignore a urine infection, and finish any course of antibiotics your doctor prescribes.

Treatment

Small stones may pass with fluids and medicines. Larger or blocking stones may need procedures such as laser or telescopic stone removal, which a urologist can explain. The right choice depends on the stone's size, position and your overall health.
