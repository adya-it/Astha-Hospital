Laparoscopic (keyhole) surgery in gynaecology: what to expect

By the Astha Hospital team · 2026-11-24

Laparoscopic surgery, often called keyhole surgery, is done through a few small cuts instead of one large incision. A thin tube with a camera and fine instruments is passed into the abdomen, and the surgeon operates while watching a magnified view on a screen.

Problems it is commonly used for

  • Ovarian cysts.
  • Fibroids.
  • Endometriosis.
  • Ectopic pregnancy.
  • Removal of the uterus (hysterectomy) when needed.
  • Investigating the cause of pelvic pain or infertility, and opening or checking the tubes.

Possible benefits compared with open surgery

  • Smaller scars.
  • Usually less pain after the operation.
  • Shorter stay in hospital and, for many patients, a quicker return to normal activity.
  • Lower risk of wound problems.

Before and after

You will have a check-up, blood tests and an anaesthesia assessment beforehand, and you will be told when to stop eating and drinking. Afterwards, shoulder or abdominal discomfort from the gas used in the procedure is common and settles within a few days. Your surgeon will tell you when to walk, drive and resume normal work.

Is it right for everyone?

Not always. Very large growths, certain previous surgeries or other medical conditions can make open surgery the safer option. The surgeon's job is to choose the safest method for you, and sometimes a procedure that begins as keyhole needs to be changed to open surgery for safety. Always ask your doctor why a particular approach is recommended.
